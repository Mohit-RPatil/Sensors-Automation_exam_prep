# Q2: Explain the working principle of RVDT (Rotary Variable Differential Transformer) in detail.

An RVDT (Rotary Variable Differential Transformer) is an electromechanical transducer that measures angular displacement. It is the rotary equivalent of the LVDT.

## Construction and Diagram

Similar to an LVDT, an RVDT consists of a primary winding and two secondary windings. However, instead of a movable linear core, an RVDT has a cam-shaped core that rotates between the windings. The angular displacement to be measured is applied to the shaft connected to this core.

## Working Principle

The working principle of an RVDT is based on mutual induction. An AC supply is connected to the primary winding, which generates an alternating magnetic field. This field induces voltages in the two secondary windings. The two secondary windings are connected in series opposition, so the output voltage is the difference between the voltages induced in the two windings.

The amount of magnetic flux linking the primary winding to each of the secondary windings depends on the angular position of the cam-shaped core.

## Operation of RVDT

The operation of an RVDT can be explained in three positions:

### 1. Null Position

When the core is at the null position, the magnetic flux is equally distributed between the two secondary windings. This results in equal voltages being induced in both windings. Since the windings are connected in series opposition, the net output voltage is zero.

### 2. Clockwise Rotation

When the shaft is rotated in the clockwise direction, the cam-shaped core causes more magnetic flux to link with one of the secondary windings (say, S1) and less with the other (S2). This results in a higher voltage being induced in S1 and a lower voltage in S2. The net output voltage is proportional to the angular displacement and is in phase with the primary voltage.

### 3. Anticlockwise Rotation

When the shaft is rotated in the anticlockwise direction, the core causes more magnetic flux to link with the S2 winding and less with the S1 winding. This results in a higher voltage being induced in S2 and a lower voltage in S1. The net output voltage is proportional to the angular displacement and is 180 degrees out of phase with the primary voltage.

The magnitude of the output voltage is proportional to the angular displacement, and the phase of the output voltage indicates the direction of rotation.

## Advantages of RVDT

*   **High Accuracy and Resolution:** RVDTs provide accurate and high-resolution measurement of angular displacement.
*   **Frictionless and Reliable:** There is no contact between the rotating core and the windings, which makes them frictionless and reliable.
*   **Robust and Durable:** RVDTs are robust and can be used in harsh environments.
*   **Long Lifespan:** Due to the absence of sliding contacts, they have a long operational life.

## Disadvantages of RVDT

*   **Limited Measurement Range:** The linear operating range of an RVDT is typically limited to about ±40 degrees.
*   **Susceptible to Magnetic Interference:** Like LVDTs, RVDTs are also susceptible to external magnetic fields.

## Applications of RVDT

RVDTs are used in a wide range of applications where angular displacement needs to be measured, including:
*   **Aerospace:** Flight control systems, throttle position feedback.
*   **Industrial Automation:** Robotics, machine tool control.
*   **Marine:** Ship and submarine control systems.
*   **Automotive:** Pedal and throttle position sensing.

# Q4: Write short notes on: GPS, LIDAR, and Ultrasonic Sensor

## 1. GPS (Global Positioning System)

The Global Positioning System (GPS) is a satellite-based navigation system that provides location and time information in all weather conditions, anywhere on or near the Earth. It is maintained by the United States government and is freely accessible to anyone with a GPS receiver.

### How it Works

The GPS system consists of three segments:

1.  **Space Segment:** A constellation of satellites orbiting the Earth. Each satellite transmits signals that contain its location and the time the signal was sent.
2.  **Control Segment:** A network of ground stations that monitor the satellites and ensure they are working correctly.
3.  **User Segment:** GPS receivers (e.g., in smartphones, cars) that receive the signals from the satellites.

A GPS receiver calculates its position by precisely timing the signals sent by at least four GPS satellites. By measuring the time it takes for the signals to travel from the satellites to the receiver, the receiver can calculate its distance from each satellite. This process is called trilateration.

### Applications

*   **Navigation:** Providing directions for cars, planes, and ships.
*   **Surveying and Mapping:** Creating accurate maps and models of the Earth's surface.
*   **Telecommunications:** Synchronizing cellular networks.
*   **Emergency Services:** Locating people in distress.
*   **Agriculture:** Precision farming.

## 2. LIDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)

LIDAR is a remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure ranges (variable distances) to the Earth. These light pulses—combined with other data recorded by the airborne system— generate precise, three-dimensional information about the shape of the Earth and its surface characteristics.

### How it Works

A LIDAR system consists of a laser, a scanner, and a specialized GPS receiver. The laser emits pulses of light, and the scanner directs these pulses towards the target. The light pulses reflect off the target and return to the sensor. The time it takes for the light to travel to the target and back is used to calculate the distance to the target.

By scanning the laser across a large area, a LIDAR system can create a detailed 3D map of the environment, known as a point cloud.

### Applications

*   **Autonomous Vehicles:** LIDAR is a key sensor for self-driving cars, providing a 3D view of the car's surroundings.
*   **Archaeology:** Creating high-resolution maps of archaeological sites.
*   **Forestry:** Measuring the height and density of forests.
*   **Urban Planning:** Creating 3D models of cities.
*   **Meteorology:** Measuring clouds and aerosols in the atmosphere.

## 3. Ultrasonic Sensor

An ultrasonic sensor is a device that measures the distance to an object using ultrasonic sound waves.

### How it Works

An ultrasonic sensor has a transmitter and a receiver. The transmitter emits a high-frequency ultrasonic sound pulse. This pulse travels through the air, and if it hits an object, it reflects off the object and travels back to the receiver.

The sensor measures the time it takes for the pulse to travel to the object and back. Since the speed of sound is known, the sensor can calculate the distance to the object using the following formula:

**Distance = (Speed of Sound x Time) / 2**

The time is divided by 2 because the sound pulse travels to the object and back.

### Applications

*   **Robotics:** Obstacle avoidance and navigation for robots.
*   **Automotive:** Parking assistance systems.
*   **Industrial Automation:** Level measurement in tanks, object detection on conveyor belts.
*   **Medical:** Medical imaging (ultrasound).
*   **Drones:** Altitude control and obstacle avoidance.

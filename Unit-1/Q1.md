# Q1: What is a sensor? Explain its classification and characteristics in detail.

A **sensor** is a device that detects and responds to some type of input from the physical environment. The specific input could be light, heat, motion, moisture, pressure, or any one of a great number of other environmental phenomena. The output is generally a signal that is converted to a human-readable display at the sensor location or transmitted electronically over a network for reading or further processing.

## Classification of Sensors

Sensors can be classified based on various criteria:

### 1. Based on Power Requirement

*   **Active Sensors:** These sensors require an external power source to operate. They work on the principle of energy conversion. They produce an output signal in response to some external stimulus.
    *   **Examples:** LVDT (Linear Variable Differential Transformer), strain gauge, etc.
*   **Passive Sensors:** These sensors do not require any external power source. They directly generate an electrical signal in response to the physical stimulus.
    *   **Examples:** Thermocouple, photodiode, piezoelectric sensor, etc.

### 2. Based on the Physical Property they Measure

*   **Temperature Sensors:** Detect temperature or heat. (e.g., thermocouples, thermistors, RTDs)
*   **Proximity Sensors:** Detect the presence of nearby objects without any physical contact. (e.g., inductive, capacitive, ultrasonic)
*   **Optical Sensors:** Detect light intensity. (e.g., photodiode, LDR)
*   **Pressure Sensors:** Measure the pressure of gases or liquids. (e.g., strain gauge, Bourdon tube)
*   **Flow Sensors:** Measure the flow rate of a fluid. (e.g., ultrasonic, electromagnetic)
*   **Level Sensors:** Detect the level of liquids and other fluids. (e.g., ultrasonic, float switches)
*   **Humidity Sensors:** Measure the amount of water vapor in the air. (e.g., capacitive, resistive)
*   **Motion Sensors:** Detect movement. (e.g., PIR, microwave)
*   **Sound Sensors:** Detect sound waves. (e.g., microphone)

### 3. Based on Output Signal

*   **Analog Sensors:** Produce a continuous output signal or voltage which is generally proportional to the quantity being measured.
    *   **Examples:** Thermocouples, LDRs, strain gauges.
*   **Digital Sensors:** Produce discrete output signals. The output is either ON or OFF.
    *   **Examples:** Proximity sensors, limit switches.

## Characteristics of Sensors

The performance of a sensor is determined by its characteristics. Some of the important characteristics are:

*   **Accuracy:** The closeness of the measured value to the true value.
*   **Precision:** The ability of the sensor to reproduce the same output for repeated measurements of the same input.
*   **Range:** The maximum and minimum values of the input that the sensor can measure.
*   **Resolution:** The smallest change in the input that can be detected by the sensor.
*   **Sensitivity:** The ratio of the change in the output of the sensor to the change in the input.
*   **Response Time:** The time taken by the sensor to respond to a change in the input.
*   **Linearity:** The degree to which the output of the sensor is directly proportional to the input over its operating range.
*   **Hysteresis:** The difference in output when the input is first increased and then decreased.
*   **Stability:** The ability of the sensor to maintain its performance characteristics over a period of time.
*   **Size and Weight:** The physical dimensions and weight of the sensor.
*   **Power Consumption:** The amount of power required by the sensor for its operation.
